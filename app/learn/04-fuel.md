# Fuel system and fuel management
Tanks, filters, fuel quality, winter fuel, and the fuel side of a fleet department.

## From tank to cylinder
1. **Tanks:** usually two, one each side, 100 to 150 gallons each (200 to 300 total). A **crossover line** connects them so they level out. The fuel gauge sender is usually in only one tank, so the gauge can move strangely after a fill until the tanks even out.
2. **Primary filter / water separator:** catches water and big dirt. Has a drain valve and often a heater.
3. **Lift pump:** pulls fuel from the tank.
4. **Secondary filter:** fine filtering.
5. **High-pressure pump and rail:** builds injection pressure.
6. **Injectors:** spray fuel into the cylinders.
7. **Return line:** extra fuel goes back to the tank.

## Fuel problems

![DEF is sold as AdBlue in Europe; same fluid](fuel-def.jpg)

- **Water in fuel:** a "water in fuel" lamp, rough running, low power. Drain the separator; replace the filter.
- **Gelling:** in cold weather, diesel wax thickens and plugs filters. Prevented by winter blend (No. 1/No. 2 blend) and anti-gel additive. A gelled truck needs heat and new filters.
- **Running out of fuel:** air gets into the system. Many engines need **priming** after refilling; a road call if the driver cannot do it.
- **Wrong fuel:** gasoline in a diesel truck, or diesel in the DEF tank. Do not start the engine. Tow.
- **Contamination:** algae (in warm, wet tanks) or bad fuel from a station. Several trucks failing after the same station is a clue.

## Fuel economy
A modern sleeper does about **6 to 8 mpg**. What moves mpg most:
- **Speed:** every 1 mph over 60 costs about 0.1 mpg.
- **Idle:** about 0.8 to 1 gallon per hour. A truck idling 8 hours a night burns 8 gallons doing nothing. APUs fix that.
- **Tire pressure and alignment.**
- **Aerodynamics:** trailer skirts, gap between tractor and trailer.
- **Driver habits:** hard acceleration, cruise use, progressive shifting.

## Fuel cards and discounts
Fleets buy fuel with fuel cards (EFS, Comdata, WEX, TCH and others). Truck stop chains (Love's, Pilot/Flying J, TA/Petro) give fleets a discount off the pump price, often "cost plus" or "retail minus". The fuel department's job is to send drivers to the cheapest stop on their route, not just the closest. Kaido's Fuel section does this.

## Fuel fraud and theft
- **Card used for another vehicle:** gallons bought are more than the tank can hold or more than the tank level rose.
- **Fuel siphoned or drained:** the tank level drops while the truck is parked.
- **Sold fuel:** a driver fills, then pumps fuel into someone else's tank.
- **Gauge problems that look like theft:** the crossover line leveling the two tanks after a fill, cold starts, parking on a slope. Always check the fuel used over the next few hundred miles before accusing anyone.

Kaido's Fuel checks page flags these and compares them with Samsara readings.

## IFTA
**IFTA (International Fuel Tax Agreement):** every quarter the fleet reports miles driven and fuel bought in each state, and pays or gets back fuel tax for the difference. Missing receipts and bad mileage data cost money. ELDs and fuel cards supply most of the data.
