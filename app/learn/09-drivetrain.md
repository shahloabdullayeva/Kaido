# Drivetrain
Clutch, transmission, driveshaft and axles: how engine power reaches the wheels.

## Path of power
Engine, **clutch**, **transmission**, **driveshaft** (with U-joints), **interaxle (power divider)**, front drive axle and rear drive axle (each with a **differential**), axle shafts, wheels.

![From the engine to the wheels](drivetrain.svg)

## Transmissions
- **Automated manual transmissions (AMT):** most new trucks. A normal gearbox with a computer doing the shifting and clutching. Eaton Endurant, Eaton UltraShift, Volvo I-Shift, Detroit DT12, PACCAR TX-12, Mack mDRIVE. Problems are often sensors, the clutch actuator, or software.
- **Manual:** 10, 13 or 18 speed (Eaton Fuller). Older and heavy-haul trucks.
- **Full automatic:** Allison. Mostly vocational trucks (dump, refuse), not highway tractors.

AMT complaints you will hear: "won't go into gear", "stuck in neutral", "shifting hard", a wrench or "F" on the gear display. First steps are often clearing codes, checking air supply (many AMTs shift with air), and doing a **clutch calibration** with software.

## Clutch
Wears out like brake linings, faster in city work. Replacing a clutch means pulling the transmission: roughly 8 to 12 labor hours. Signs: slipping (revs climb, truck does not), burning smell, engagement faults on AMTs.

## Driveshaft and U-joints
**U-joints** let the shaft bend as the suspension moves. They need grease at PMs. A dry U-joint will clunk, vibrate, then break; a broken driveshaft can damage the air tanks, fuel tanks or the road behind. **Driveline vibration** at certain speeds points here or to a bad carrier bearing.

## Axles and differentials
- **Gear ratio** (for example 2.47, 2.64, 3.36): lower numbers mean lower engine rpm at highway speed and better mpg. Fixed at the factory.
- **Differential lock (diff lock):** the driver can lock the wheels together for traction on ice or mud. Must be off on dry roads or it damages the axle.
- **Interaxle lock (power divider lock):** locks the two drive axles together.
- **Axle oil:** checked at PMs, changed on a long interval (often 250,000 to 500,000 miles with synthetic). Leaks at the pinion seal and axle shaft seals are common.

## Wheel ends
Hub, bearings, seal, and the brake drum. A leaking hub seal throws oil on the brake linings. A hot hub after driving is a warning sign of a bearing going bad. Many fleets use "unitized" hubs that are sealed for life.
