## Overview
The job is triage, vendors, price and paperwork, not fixing trucks. Learn it in this order: the breakdown call, the words, the four systems behind most calls, then the planning side. The day by day plan is in Study schedule.

**What a truck is made of, in the order to learn it.** A semi is a tractor (the truck) pulling a trailer. Most breakdown calls come from the first four rows.

| System | Main parts | Usual problems |
|---|---|---|
| Emissions (aftertreatment) | DPF (soot filter), DOC, SCR, DEF (blue fluid), EGR | Number one cause of breakdowns. DPF clogs and needs a regen (burning off soot), DEF sensor faults, then a derate: the computer limits speed, anywhere from 5 to 55 mph depending on the fault |
| Engine | Cummins X15, Detroit DD15, PACCAR MX 13, Volvo D13; turbo, injectors, oil, coolant, belts | Oil or coolant leaks, overheating, turbo failure, check engine light |
| Brakes | Air brakes: compressor, air dryer, air tanks, brake chambers, slack adjusters, drums or rotors | Air leaks, brakes out of adjustment (the most common DOT violation), worn shoes or pads |
| Tires and wheels | Steer (2 front), drive (8 middle), trailer (8 back) | Blowouts, low tread (legal minimum 4/32" steer, 2/32" others), loose lug nuts |
| Electrical | Batteries, alternator, starter, lights, ABS, ECM (engine computer) | Dead batteries, ABS light, lights out (easy DOT tickets) |
| Drivetrain | Transmission (mostly automated: Eaton, Volvo I Shift, Detroit DT12), clutch, driveshaft, U joints, differentials | Won't shift, clutch wear, driveline vibration |
| Steering and suspension | Steering box, tie rods, kingpins, air bags, shocks | Pulling to one side, uneven tire wear, alignment |
| Cooling and AC | Radiator, water pump, AC compressor, condenser | Overheating, no AC |
| Fifth wheel | The plate the trailer hooks onto | Wear, needs grease |
| Trailer | Brakes, tires, lights, landing gear, doors; on a reefer the cooling unit (Thermo King or Carrier) with its own fuel tank | Lights, tires, reefer alarms |

## Types of maintenance

- **PM A, PM B, PM C:** scheduled services. A is an oil change plus inspection, roughly every 15,000 to 25,000 miles. B and C add filters, fluids and more checks. Details in Preventive maintenance.
- **DOT annual inspection:** required by law every 12 months; a sticker goes on the truck.
- **PTI / DVIR:** the driver's daily check (Kaido already does PTI).
- **Roadside inspection:** a police or DOT check. Failing it means OOS (out of service) and the truck can't move.
- **Breakdown / road service:** fixed on the road, or towed to a shop.
- **Warranty and recalls:** always ask "is this under warranty?" before paying.

## The words you'll hear every day

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| Unit number | The fleet's own name for the truck |
| VIN | 17 character vehicle ID; decodes to brand, year, engine |
| Work order / RO | The repair job record (RO = repair order) |
| PO | Purchase order: your approval number for the job |
| Estimate | The price quoted before work starts |
| Labor rate | What the shop charges per hour |
| Parts and labor | The two halves of every invoice |
| Core charge | A deposit on a rebuildable part, refunded when the old part goes back |
| Mobile mechanic vs shop | Mechanic comes to the truck, or the truck goes to the shop |
| Tow | Truck can't move; priced by hookup plus miles |
| Derate | Computer cuts power and speed to force a repair |
| Regen | Burning soot out of the DPF |
| Fault code | SPN (which part) plus FMI (what kind of failure) |
| Downtime | Hours or days the truck isn't earning |
| Cost per mile | Total cost divided by miles driven; the number fleets live by |

**What the job actually is.** A driver calls with a problem. You ask: unit number, location, what happened, which dash lights are on, fault codes, is it derated, is it safe to drive, is it loaded, and when is the delivery appointment. You find a shop or mobile mechanic nearby, get an estimate and approve it after checking price and warranty, track it until it's fixed, and close the work order with the invoice. The full call flow is in Breakdowns and road service.

**The planning half.** Keep PMs and DOT annuals from going overdue, spot trucks that break down over and over, and keep costs down.
