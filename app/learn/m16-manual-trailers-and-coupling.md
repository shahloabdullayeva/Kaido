# Trailers and coupling
From the Fleet From Scratch manual.

## Trailer types

| Type | What it is | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Dry van | Enclosed box, usually 53 ft | The most common trailer. Doors, floor, roof and lights are the usual repairs |
| Reefer | Refrigerated van with a cooling unit on the front (Thermo King or Carrier) | The reefer unit has its own small diesel engine, its own fuel tank and its own maintenance. Reefer fuel is often bought separately on fuel cards |
| Flatbed | Open flat deck; loads are strapped or chained | Straps, chains, tarps and securement are inspected |
| Step deck / lowboy | Flatbeds with a lower deck for tall loads | Heavy haul |
| Tanker | Liquids | Special inspections |
| Intermodal chassis | Frame that carries shipping containers | Often owned by someone else |

## How the tractor and trailer connect (coupling)

- **Fifth wheel:** the plate on the tractor. It has locking **jaws** that close around the trailer's **kingpin**. It needs grease, and the locking mechanism is checked on every pre-trip. A bad lock can let a trailer drop off the truck.
- **Sliding fifth wheel:** can be moved forward or back to balance weight between the steer and drive axles.
- **Gladhands and air lines:** connect tractor air to the trailer (red emergency, blue service).
- **7 way cord:** electrical power for trailer lights and ABS.
- **Landing gear:** the two legs at the front of the trailer that hold it up when no tractor is under it. Cranked up and down by hand.
- **Trailer tandem slider:** the trailer axles can slide forward or back to balance weight, locked with pins.
- **Mud flaps, DOT reflective tape, under ride guard (the rear bumper), and side skirts** (aero panels) are all trailer items that get inspected or damaged.

**Trailer repairs you'll see:** lights and wiring, brakes and slack adjusters, tires, air leaks, landing gear, door hinges and seals, floor damage, reefer unit repairs, and annual inspections (trailers need their own).
