# Breakdown calls
A step-by-step playbook for when a driver calls with a problem.

## First minute: safety
1. Is the driver safe? Off the road, hazards on, triangles out (within 10 minutes, at 10, 100 and 200 feet behind).
2. Is anyone hurt? Is there fire, smoke or fuel leaking? If yes, 911 first.
3. Is the truck in a dangerous spot (live lane, curve, bridge)? Police help may be needed.

## Questions to ask, every time
| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| Unit number (and trailer number) | Pulls up the truck, its history and warranty |
| Exact location: highway, mile marker, direction, nearest exit, or the pin from the ELD | Road service needs it to find the truck |
| What happened, in the driver's words | Symptoms before codes |
| Which lamps are on, and the codes | Tells you severity |
| Can the truck move? At what speed? | Derate or not, tow or not |
| Loaded or empty? What is in the trailer (reefer, hazmat)? | Urgency, hazmat rules, reefer fuel |
| Delivery appointment time | Whether to plan a recovery or a power swap |
| Driver's hours left | A long repair may become a rest break |
| Photos or a short video | Saves a lot of guessing |

## Decide the path

![Which way a breakdown call goes](breakdown-flow.svg)

1. **Driver can fix it:** reset a breaker, drain the water separator, do a parked regen, replace a gladhand seal, refill DEF. Talk them through it.
2. **Drive to a shop:** truck is safe and moving at normal speed. Pick a shop on the route.
3. **Mobile road service:** truck cannot move but the fix is simple (tire, air line, belt, battery, sensor).
4. **Tow:** truck cannot move and the fix needs a shop (engine, transmission, major aftertreatment, accident).
5. **Recover the load:** if the repair takes long, send another truck to take the trailer (**power swap** or **load recovery**) so the delivery is not missed.

## Getting road service
- Use the fleet's preferred vendors first; they have agreed rates.
- Directories: truck stop service networks (Love's Truck Care, TA Truck Service), FleetNet, Find Truck Service, Breakdown Inc, and the dealer network.
- Ask for: **ETA, service call fee, hourly rate, mileage charge, minimum hours, after-hours rate, parts markup, payment method.**
- Give the vendor the exact location, the unit description, the problem, the tire size if it is a tire, and the driver's phone.

## Tows
- Ask: hook-up fee, per-mile rate, the tow truck class (heavy wrecker for a loaded semi), waiting time, and whether they can tow with the trailer attached.
- A loaded tractor-trailer tow can cost **$1,000 to $5,000+**. A tow of just the tractor is cheaper.
- Choose the destination before the tow: the dealer for warranty work, or a trusted shop. Towing twice costs twice.

## Approving the estimate
1. Get the estimate in writing: parts, labor hours, rate, shop supplies, taxes.
2. Check the **labor hours** against what the job normally takes (ask a senior mechanic or a labor guide).
3. Check **warranty**: engine, aftertreatment and many parts carry warranty. A dealer can file it; an independent shop may not.
4. Ask for the **old parts** back or photos of them.
5. Approve in writing (email or text) with a PO number or a not-to-exceed amount.

## While it is in the shop
- Check in every few hours: diagnosis done? parts available? ETA?
- Keep dispatch and the customer updated about delivery time.
- Track the driver's situation: hotel if the repair is overnight, per fleet policy.

## Closing the case
1. Get the final invoice. Compare it with the estimate. Question anything added.
2. Make sure the shop cleared codes and test drove.
3. Log a work order with the invoice, the parts, and the cause.
4. Note follow-ups: warranty claims, parts to watch, a PM that is coming due.
5. If the same truck breaks down for the same thing again, it goes on the "watch" list.
