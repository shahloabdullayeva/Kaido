# Engine
How a diesel makes power, the parts that fail, and what the shop is talking about.

## How a diesel works
A diesel squeezes air so hard it gets hot (about 1,000 °F), then sprays fuel into it. The fuel lights by itself from the heat; there are no spark plugs. That is why diesels need strong batteries and sometimes a grid heater to start in the cold: a cold engine cannot heat the air enough.

A Class 8 engine is usually **13 to 16 litres**, 6 cylinders in a row, 400 to 605 horsepower, and should last **750,000 to 1,000,000+ miles** before an **overhaul** (in-frame or out-of-frame rebuild).

## Main parts

![How a turbocharger works (animation)](engine-turbo-anim.gif)

| Part | What it does | Typical trouble |
|---|---|---|
| Turbocharger | Uses exhaust to spin a fan that pushes more air in | Oil leaks, whistling, low power, smoke, actuator faults on VGT turbos |
| Charge air cooler (CAC) | Cools the air from the turbo before it enters the engine | Cracks and boot leaks cause low power and boost codes |
| Injectors | Spray fuel into each cylinder | Misfire, smoke, fuel in the oil, rough idle. Sold in sets, expensive |
| High-pressure fuel pump | Builds injection pressure (up to 30,000+ psi) | Hard start, low rail pressure codes |
| EGR valve and cooler | Sends some exhaust back in to lower emissions | Coolant leaks into exhaust, white smoke, overheating, soot buildup |
| Water pump | Moves coolant | Leaks from the weep hole, bearing noise, overheating |
| Oil cooler | Keeps oil temperature down | Coolant in oil or oil in coolant |
| Head gasket | Seals the cylinder head | Coolant loss, overheating, pressure in the cooling system |
| Engine brake (Jake brake) | Turns the engine into an air compressor to slow the truck | Not working on one or more cylinders, solenoid faults |
| ECM | The engine computer | Software updates, wiring, sensor faults |
| Belts and tensioner | Drive the alternator, AC compressor, fan, water pump | Squeal, cracks, thrown belt (truck stops charging or overheats) |
| Fan clutch | Turns the cooling fan on when hot | Stuck on (wastes fuel, loud) or stuck off (overheats) |

## Fluids and filters
- **Engine oil:** 10W-30 or 15W-40 meeting API **CK-4** or **FA-4** (FA-4 only where the engine maker allows it). A Class 8 sump holds about 10 to 13 gallons. Change intervals are usually 25,000 to 75,000 miles depending on engine, oil and duty, often tied to oil analysis.
- **Oil filter(s):** changed with every oil change.
- **Fuel filters:** a primary (with water separator) and a secondary. Usually every PM or every second PM. Bad fuel or water plugs them and causes low power.
- **Air filter:** checked by a restriction gauge, not by mileage. Change when the indicator says so.
- **Crankcase filter (CCV/breather):** on many engines, part of the PM schedule.
- **Coolant:** extended-life (ELC/OAT), tested at PMs, changed around 600,000 miles or 12,000 hours with an extender at the halfway point.

## Oil analysis
A small oil sample sent to a lab. It shows **fuel dilution** (injector problems), **coolant** (glycol, from a leaking oil cooler or head gasket), **soot**, and wear metals like iron, copper and lead. Good fleets sample at every oil change on high-value trucks. A sudden jump in one metal is often the first warning of a failure.

## Smoke colour
- **Black:** too much fuel or not enough air. Turbo, CAC leak, air filter, injector.
- **White:** unburned fuel on a cold start, or coolant burning (EGR cooler, head gasket) if it smells sweet and does not clear.
- **Blue:** burning oil. Turbo seals, worn rings, valve seals.

## Overhaul
- **In-frame overhaul:** engine stays in the truck; liners, pistons, rings, bearings replaced. Roughly $20,000 to $35,000.
- **Out-of-frame:** engine removed and rebuilt. More thorough and more expensive.
- **Reman engine:** swap in a factory remanufactured engine. Often $40,000+.

When a truck needs an engine, the fleet decides between repair, reman, or selling the truck. Mileage, age and the truck's history decide it.
