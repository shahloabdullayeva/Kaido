# Shops, invoices and warranty
How repair pricing works, how to read an invoice, and how not to overpay.

## How shops charge
- **Labor rate:** per hour. Roughly $120 to $200+ per hour, higher at dealers and after hours. Mobile service adds a call-out fee and mileage.
- **Book hours (flat rate):** many repairs have a standard time in a labor guide. A shop charges the book time even if the tech is faster. Asking "what is the book time?" is fair.
- **Diagnostic time:** usually 1 to 2 hours to find a problem. Approve it separately from the repair.
- **Parts:** marked up from the shop's cost. 20 to 50% is normal; dealers charge list price.
- **Core charge:** a deposit on rebuildable parts (starters, alternators, turbos, injectors, DPFs). Refunded when the old part goes back. Make sure the core credit appears.
- **Shop supplies:** a small percentage for rags, cleaners, small hardware. Should be modest.
- **Environmental / disposal fee:** for oil, coolant, tires.
- **Taxes:** depend on the state.

## Reading an invoice
1. Unit number, VIN and mileage are right? Mistakes here break warranty and records.
2. **Complaint, cause, correction:** a good invoice says what the driver reported, what the tech found, and what was done.
3. Labor lines: hours times rate. Does each line match the work?
4. Parts lines: part numbers, quantities, prices. Duplicate lines happen.
5. Core charges and their credits.
6. Was the estimate exceeded? Was that approved?
7. Warranty parts should not be billed to the fleet.

## Red flags
- Labor far above what the job normally takes.
- Vague lines like "misc parts" or "diagnosis" with many hours and no finding.
- Parts replaced that do not match the complaint ("while we were there" items not approved).
- The same repair on the same truck a few weeks later (ask for warranty on the repair itself).
- Tow plus storage fees adding up while waiting for approval.

## Warranty
- **Base vehicle warranty:** usually 1 to 2 years.
- **Engine and aftertreatment:** often longer (for example 2 to 5 years, by miles). Extended warranties are common.
- **Parts warranty:** parts installed by a shop often carry 12 months; ask.
- **Repair warranty:** the shop's guarantee on its work.
- **Recalls and campaigns:** free fixes from the maker. The dealer looks them up by VIN. Check before every dealer visit.

Warranty work must usually be done at an authorized dealer. If an independent shop replaces a warranty part, the fleet usually pays. **Check warranty before approving any engine, aftertreatment or transmission repair.**

## Building a shop network
- Note which shops are fair, fast, and honest. Record their labor rate and contact.
- Use national accounts where the fleet has them (Love's, TA, dealer groups).
- Pay attention to downtime, not only price: a cheap shop that keeps a truck 5 days costs more than a fair one that keeps it 1 day.

## More detail
Two made up example invoices, read line by line. The prices are realistic but invented; the point is what to check on each line.

**Example 1: PM service at a shop.** Unit 1045, 2016 Freightliner Cascadia, Cummins ISX15, 612,450 miles.

| Line | Qty | Price | Total | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engine oil 15W-40 CK-4 | 12 gal | $18.50 | $222.00 | Gallons close to the engine's capacity (10 to 15). CK-4 is right for this engine |
| Oil filter | 1 | $45.00 | $45.00 | One filter; some engines have two |
| Fuel filter, primary (with water separator) | 1 | $38.00 | $38.00 | Changed at PM |
| Fuel filter, secondary | 1 | $42.00 | $42.00 | Changed at PM |
| Chassis lube | 1 | $45.00 | $45.00 | Grease job, normal at every PM A |
| Labor, PM A | 1.0 hr | $149.00 | $149.00 | About an hour is normal for a PM A |
| Shop supplies | 1 | $15.00 | $15.00 | Common small fee |
| Oil disposal fee | 1 | $8.00 | $8.00 | Common small fee |
| **Total before tax** |  |  | **$564.00** | Inside the usual $300 to $600 PM A range |

Also check the header: the unit number, VIN and mileage match the truck, and the mileage is higher than on the last invoice. If the oil changes show up far apart in miles, PMs are being skipped.

**Example 2: roadside tire call.** Unit 2210, flat on the right rear outer drive, I-40 mile marker 212.

| Line | Qty | Price | Total | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Service call | 1 | $195.00 | $195.00 | Normal call out fee |
| Mileage | 24 mi | $4.00 | $96.00 | Distance from the vendor's shop to the truck. Check it on a map |
| Tire 295/75R22.5 drive, used Grade A, position RRO | 1 | $260.00 | $260.00 | Size matches the other tires; position matches the driver's report; used, so priced below new |
| Mount and dismount | 1 | $45.00 | $45.00 | Normal |
| Valve stem | 1 | $12.00 | $12.00 | Normal with a tire change |
| Tire disposal | 1 | $10.00 | $10.00 | Normal |
| Labor | 1.0 hr | $160.00 | $160.00 | Question: mount and dismount is already charged, so what was this hour for? Could be fair (travel time, airing up, torquing), but worth asking |
| **Total before tax** |  |  | **$778.00** |  |

What makes an invoice trustworthy: every line has a reason, the tire says size, position and new or used, the labor matches the job, and the totals add up. When something looks off, ask the vendor before paying; they usually explain or correct it.
