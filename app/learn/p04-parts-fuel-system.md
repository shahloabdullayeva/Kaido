# Fuel system
Each part: where it is, what it does, how it fails, and a rough installed price.

## Parts
| Part | Where / what it does | Signs of trouble | Installed (approx) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fuel tanks | Side mounted, usually two, 100 to 150 gal each | Leaks, dents, loose straps | $1,000 to $2,500 each |
| Fuel tank straps and brackets | Hold the tanks on | Broken strap (inspection item) | $150 to $400 |
| Fuel cap | Seals the tank | Missing or leaking cap | $20 to $60 part |
| Fuel sending unit | Tells the fuel gauge the level | Gauge wrong | $200 to $500 |
| Fuel lines and fittings | Carry fuel tank to engine and back | Leaks, rubbed through lines | $150 to $600 |
| Fuel water separator | Removes water from fuel | Water in bowl, rough running | $150 to $400 |
| Primary and secondary fuel filters | Remove dirt | Low power, stalling | $50 to $150 parts; done at PM |
| Fuel filter housing / module | Holds the filters, often with a heater | Leaks, air in fuel | $500 to $1,500 |
| Lift (transfer) pump | Pulls fuel from the tanks | Hard start, low fuel pressure | $300 to $800 |
| High pressure fuel pump | Makes injection pressure | No start, low power; expensive | $2,500 to $6,000 |
| Common rail and high pressure lines | Hold and carry high pressure fuel | Leaks (dangerous at this pressure) | $500 to $2,000 |
| Fuel injectors | Spray fuel into each cylinder | Misfire, knock, smoke | $2,000 to $4,000 set of six; $400 to $900 each |
| Fuel pressure sensor | Monitors rail pressure | Codes, derate | $150 to $500 |
| Fuel heater | Warms fuel in cold weather | Gelling in winter | $200 to $600 |
| Fuel primer pump | Removes air after filter changes | Hard start after service | $100 to $300 |
