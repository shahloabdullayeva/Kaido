# Steering and suspension
What holds the truck up, keeps it straight, and keeps the ride smooth.

## Steering
- **Steering gear (box):** hydraulic power steering, driven by a pump on the engine.
- **Pitman arm, drag link, tie rod and tie rod ends:** connect the box to the front wheels.
- **Kingpins and bushings:** the pivots the front wheels turn on. Worn kingpins give loose, wandering steering and odd tire wear. They need grease at every PM.
- **Power steering fluid and hoses:** leaks, whining pump, heavy steering.

Loose steering (too much "play" at the wheel) is a DOT violation and a safety risk. Check it at every PM.

## Suspension

![Leaf springs held to the axle with U-bolts](susp-leaf.jpg)

- **Front:** usually leaf springs with shock absorbers.
- **Rear (drive):** almost always **air ride**: air bags (air springs), a **height control valve** (leveling valve), shocks, and torque rods.
- **Trailer:** air ride or spring (leaf) suspension.

Common faults:
| Part | Fault |
|---|---|
| Air bags | Leaking or blown bag; truck sits low on one side |
| Height control valve | Truck rides too high or too low |
| Shocks | Leaking oil; bouncy ride; cupped tires |
| Leaf springs | Broken leaf. A DOT out-of-service item |
| Torque rods and bushings | Clunk, axle walking, tire wear |
| U-bolts | Loose or broken, axle shifts |

## Frame
Two long steel rails. Cracks near crossmembers or suspension brackets are serious and need welding to the maker's procedure. Frame damage can come from overloading or accidents.

## Alignment
Front-end alignment (toe, camber, caster) and **axle alignment** (making sure the drive axles are square to the frame). A truck that "dog tracks" (rear end runs offset from the front) has misaligned axles. Bad alignment shows first on the tires.
