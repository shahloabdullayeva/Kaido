# Steering and suspension
From the Fleet From Scratch manual.

## Overview
**Suspension** holds the truck up, absorbs bumps and keeps the tires on the road. There are four main types:

| Type | How it works | Good at | Downside | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leaf spring | Stacked curved steel strips that flex | Durable, cheap, low maintenance, heavy loads | Rough ride | Steer axles of most trucks, older trucks, some trailers |
| Air ride | Rubber air bags filled from the truck's air system | Smooth ride, protects cargo, adjustable height | More parts to fail: bags, leveling valves, lines | Drive axles of almost every highway tractor, most modern trailers |
| Torsion bar | A steel bar that twists under load | Compact | Rare on semis | Some trailers |
| Walking beam | A beam connecting the two axles that pivots in the middle | Very tough, handles rough ground | Stiff ride | Construction and off road trucks |

**Suspension parts to know:** air bags (air springs), **leveling valve** (keeps ride height right by adding or dumping air), shock absorbers, torque rods (hold the axles in place), U bolts (clamp springs to axles), bushings (rubber pieces at every pivot point), and spring hangers. Leaking air bags, broken leaf springs and worn bushings all show up in inspections. Worn suspension also causes uneven tire wear.

**Steering** turns the front wheels.

| Part | What it does | Common problems |
|---|---|---|
| Steering gear (steering box) | Turns the driver's wheel motion into movement at the wheels, with power steering assist | Leaks, loose steering |
| Power steering pump and fluid | Makes steering easy | Whining, leaks, hard steering |
| Pitman arm, drag link, tie rod ends | Linkage connecting the gear to both front wheels | Worn joints = loose, wandering steering. DOT inspectors check for play |
| Kingpins | The pivot the front wheel turns on | Worn kingpins = shaky steering, uneven tire wear. Greased at PM |
| Wheel alignment | How straight the wheels point | Bad alignment eats steer tires fast |

**Frame.** Under everything are two long steel **frame rails** joined by crossmembers. Cracks or rust in the frame are serious; a cracked frame can put the truck out of service.
