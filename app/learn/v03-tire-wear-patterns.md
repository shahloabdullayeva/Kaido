# Tire wear patterns
What uneven tire wear looks like, what causes it and what to fix, with drawings.
## How to look at a tire
Run your hand across the tread (not along it). Feel for edges that are sharp one way and smooth the other way, for dips, and for steps. Measure tread depth with a gauge in the main grooves at three spots across and at two or three spots around the tire. Legal minimum in the US: **4/32"** on steer tires, **2/32"** on drive and trailer tires.
> tip: The drawings show two views: the top is the tread as if you cut the tire across, the bottom is the tread seen from above. Dark is deep tread, light is worn.

## Center wear
@svg wear/center.svg | Center wear: the middle ribs are lower than the shoulders.
::: issue Center wear | watch
looks: The center ribs are lower than the outer ribs. On a tread gauge the center reads 2-4/32" less than the shoulders.
why: Over-inflation, mostly. Also wide-base single tires run on too high pressure, and drive tires spinning (wheel slip).
check: Pressure cold with a gauge. Compare to the load and inflation table for that tire size and load.
do: Set the pressure to the right number. Rotate or move the tire if wear is bad.
cost: Nothing to fix; a lost tire early costs $400-700 for a drive tire.
:::
## Both shoulders worn
@svg wear/shoulders.svg | Both shoulders worn: the outer ribs are lower than the center.
::: issue Shoulder wear on both sides | watch
looks: Both outer ribs worn down, center still deep. Tire looks rounded at the edges.
why: Under-inflation or overloading. The tire flexes too much and the shoulders scrub. Also a slow leak.
check: Pressure cold. Look for a nail, a leaking valve stem or a bad valve cap. Check axle weights.
do: Fix the leak, set the pressure. Under-inflated duals also overheat and blow out, so find the cause today.
dot: A tire below 50% of the pressure on its sidewall (or flat) is out of service.
:::
## One shoulder worn
@svg wear/one-shoulder.svg | One shoulder worn: the tread slopes from one side to the other.
::: issue One-sided shoulder wear | soon
looks: Tread worn on one edge only, sloping across. On steer tires usually the outside edge of both tires (toe-in) or one tire only (camber).
why: Steer: toe out of spec, camber out (bent axle or worn kingpins), worn steering parts. Drive and trailer: axle not square (thrust angle), bent axle, worn suspension bushings.
check: Ask for an alignment check, all axles. Check kingpin play and tie rod ends.
do: Full alignment ($150-350 at a shop, $250-500 for a full tractor 3-axle alignment). Replace worn steering parts.
:::
## Feathering
@svg wear/feathering.svg | Feathering: each rib is worn on a slant, like a saw blade.
::: issue Feathering (sawtooth across ribs) | soon
looks: Each rib is high on one side and low on the other. Running your hand one way feels smooth, the other way feels sharp.
feel: Driver may feel a light pull and steering that never centers.
why: Toe out of adjustment on the steer axle, bent tie rod, or a drive axle that is not parallel.
do: Alignment. Do not wait: feathering eats a steer tire in a few thousand miles.
:::
## Cupping or scalloping
@svg wear/cupping.svg | Cupping: dips worn into the tread at intervals around the tire.
::: issue Cupping or scalloping | soon
looks: Scoops or dips worn into the tread every few inches around the tire, like a wavy edge.
hear: A rhythmic "wub-wub" drone that changes with speed.
feel: Vibration through the steering wheel.
why: Worn shocks, out-of-balance tire and wheel, loose wheel bearings, worn kingpins or steering parts.
check: Shocks (oil wet or bouncing), wheel bearing end play, balance.
do: Replace shocks, balance, fix the bearing. Cupped tires seldom wear flat again: move to a trailer position or replace.
:::
## Flat spot
@svg wear/flat-spot.svg | Flat spot: one patch worn down across the full width.
::: issue Flat spot | soon
looks: One area worn bald across the whole width, rest of the tire fine. Sometimes rubber is torn or blue-burned.
feel: Thump or hop at low speed.
why: Skid from a locked wheel: a brake that grabs, a stuck spring brake, a trailer pulled with brakes locked, ABS not working.
check: The brake on that wheel and the ABS. Look for a trailer that was moved with the air not connected.
do: Replace the tire if through to the belts. Fix the brake.
:::
## Heel-toe wear
@svg wear/heel-toe.svg | Heel-toe wear: each tread block worn into a step.
::: issue Heel-toe (step) wear | watch
looks: Each tread block is higher at one end than the other, like stairs, all the way around.
why: Normal on drive tires with block tread, worse with high torque and lots of braking. Made worse by low pressure.
do: Rotate the drive tires. Check pressure.
:::
## Diagonal wipe
@svg wear/diagonal.svg | Diagonal wipe: a worn band running across the tread at an angle.
::: issue Diagonal wipe | watch
looks: One or more worn bands running at an angle across the tread. Very common on trailer tires.
why: Trailer axle out of alignment, a tire mounted on a dual with a much different diameter, or the tire running mostly empty (light loads, spread axles).
do: Check the trailer alignment and match the dual tires in size and wear. Flip the tire on the wheel if bad.
:::
## River wear
@svg wear/river.svg | River wear: a narrow worn line along the edge of a rib.
::: issue River or rib edge wear | watch
looks: A thin worn line running along the edge of a rib, like a small river. Usually on steer and trailer tires with rib tread.
why: Normal on long highway runs. Watch it but it usually does not grow.
do: Nothing unless it spreads to the full rib.
:::
## Damage you can see
::: issue Sidewall bulge or bubble | stop
looks: A round bump on the sidewall, like a blister, usually 1-3 inches. You can see it better if you look along the side of the tire.
why: The cords inside are broken from hitting a curb or pothole, or from running low.
do: Replace now. It can blow at any moment.
dot: Out of service on any position.
cost: Steer tire $450-750 mounted; drive $400-650; trailer $300-500.
:::
::: issue Cuts showing cords or belts | stop
looks: A cut deep enough to see fabric or shiny steel wire.
do: Replace. Exposed belts or cords are out of service.
:::
::: issue Tread separation | stop
looks: A lump or wave in the tread, the tread lifting at an edge, or a section of tread loose. Driver may feel a thump.
why: Heat from under-inflation, a bad retread, or damage.
do: Stop and replace. These come apart on the road and the pieces ("gators") cause crashes and body damage.
:::
::: issue Duals touching | soon
looks: The two tires of a dual pair touch in the middle, rubber rubbed and shiny, sometimes smoke.
why: One tire flat or low, wrong wheel, mismatched tires.
do: Fix pressure today. Tires touching is out of service.
:::
::: issue Mismatched dual tire sizes | watch
looks: The two tires on one side look different heights. Measure with a tape around the tire.
why: Mixed brands or one new and one worn tire.
do: Match within 1/4" of diameter (3/4" of circumference). The bigger tire carries all the load and wears fast.
:::
::: issue Weather checking (dry rot) | watch
looks: Fine cracks on the sidewall or between tread blocks, like dried mud.
why: Age, sun, ozone. Common on trailers that sit.
do: Replace if cracks reach the cords or the tire is over 6-7 years old (DOT date code on the sidewall: last 4 digits are week and year).
:::
::: issue Run-flat damage | stop
looks: A grey, powdery or wrinkled band around the inside of the sidewall, crumbs of rubber inside.
why: Driven flat. Often the inside tire of a dual that went flat without anyone noticing.
do: Scrap the tire. Never put it back in service.
:::
