## Overview
A fleet is any company that runs several trucks, and fleet maintenance is the job of keeping those trucks legal, safe and on the road for the fewest dollars per mile.

## Who's who

- **Carrier:** the trucking company. Each one has a USDOT number (its ID with the federal regulator, FMCSA) and usually an MC number (its permission to haul for hire).
- **Company driver:** drives the carrier's truck. **Owner operator:** owns their own truck and leases onto a carrier, so they often pay for their own repairs.
- **Dispatcher:** plans loads and tells drivers where to go. Cares about the truck only when it is late or broken.
- **Fleet manager / maintenance manager:** decides when trucks get serviced, approves repairs, watches costs.
- **Road service / breakdown coordinator:** when a truck breaks down on the road, finds a shop or mobile mechanic, gets a quote, approves it and arranges payment. This is the part closest to your fuel card work.
- **Shop / vendor:** the repair place. Dealers (Freightliner dealer, Peterbilt dealer) fix brand specific things; independent shops and truck stops (TA Petro, Love's, Pilot) do general repairs and tires.

## How a truck is identified

- **Unit number:** the fleet's own name for the truck, like Unit 1045. Trailers have their own numbers.
- **VIN:** the 17 character vehicle ID, stamped on the truck and on the registration. Shops use it to look up the exact engine and parts.
- **Plate and state:** needed for roadside inspections and tolls.

## What the maintenance side actually does all day

- Keeps every unit on its preventive maintenance (PM) schedule.
- Reads driver inspection reports (DVIRs) and gets reported defects fixed.
- Handles breakdowns: diagnose over the phone, find a vendor, approve the repair, pay.
- Checks invoices against what was actually needed.
- Keeps records for DOT: annual inspections, repairs, tire and brake work.

**The one number fleets care about:** cost per mile (CPM). Fuel is the biggest cost, then driver pay, then maintenance and tires. Every decision in this guide, from how often to change oil to whether to buy a used tire, is really a cost per mile decision.
