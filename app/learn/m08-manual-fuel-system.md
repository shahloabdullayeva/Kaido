# Fuel system
From the Fleet From Scratch manual.

## Overview
**Fuel system.** Diesel goes from the fuel tanks (usually two, one on each side, 100 to 150 gallons each) through filters to a high pressure pump, then to the injectors. Modern engines use **common rail** injection, where fuel is held at extremely high pressure in a shared rail and each injector opens electronically.

| Part | What it does | Common problems |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel tanks | Hold the diesel | Leaks, damaged straps, water in fuel |
| Fuel/water separator | Removes water from fuel (water destroys injectors) | Needs draining; full separator = rough running |
| Fuel filters | Remove dirt; there are usually two stages | Clogged filter = low power, stalling. Changed at every PM |
| Fuel pump (high pressure) | Pressurizes fuel for the injectors | Expensive failure, often caused by bad or contaminated fuel |
| Fuel injectors | Spray fuel into each cylinder, electronically controlled | Leaking or sticking = misfire, smoke, knock. Often replaced as a set of 6 |
| Fuel lines | Carry fuel | Leaks, rubbing through on the frame |

**Gelling.** In cold weather diesel turns waxy and clogs filters, stopping the truck. Drivers use winter blend fuel or anti gel additive. You'll see this as a winter road service call and sometimes as additive purchases on fuel cards.
