# Costs and numbers
The numbers managers look at, and what is normal.

## Cost per mile
The main measure in trucking. **Maintenance and repair cost per mile** for a newer sleeper fleet is often **$0.15 to $0.25 per mile**, and climbs as trucks age past 400,000 to 500,000 miles. Tires add a few cents per mile. Fuel is the biggest cost, usually **$0.50 to $0.70+ per mile**.

To work it out: total maintenance cost for a truck (or the fleet) divided by miles driven in the same period.

## Downtime
Days a truck could not run because of repairs. Each day down loses revenue (often **$500 to $1,000+ per day** for a sleeper truck). Managers often care more about downtime than repair price.

## Other numbers
| Number | What it shows |
|---|---|
| PM compliance | Share of PMs done on time. Aim for 90%+ |
| Road calls per 100,000 mi | How often trucks break down on the road |
| Repeat repairs | Same fix on the same truck within 30 to 90 days. Points to bad diagnosis or bad parts |
| Tire cost per mile | Tire program health |
| Vehicle maintenance BASIC | DOT view of maintenance quality |
| Average age of the fleet | Older fleets cost more to maintain |
| Idle percentage | Fuel waste and extra engine hours |
| MPG by truck | Spots trucks with mechanical problems (a sudden drop in mpg often means an engine or aftertreatment issue) |

## Repair or replace
A truck gets more expensive to keep each year past roughly 500,000 to 700,000 miles. Fleets compare the yearly maintenance cost and downtime with the cost of a new or newer truck (payment, warranty, better mpg). One big repair (an engine or a transmission) on an old truck often triggers the decision.

## Budgeting
Monthly or quarterly maintenance budgets are built from cost per mile times expected miles, plus planned big items (tires, DOT annuals, DPF cleanings). Tracking actual spend against it every month avoids surprises.
