# Tires and wheels
Positions, sizes, tread rules, pressure, recaps and road service.

## Positions and types

![Steer, drive and trailer positions, inner and outer](tire-positions.svg)

| Position | Count | Tire type | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steer | 2 | Steer (rib) tread | Most important for safety. New tires only on many fleets, no recaps |
| Drive | 8 | Drive (lug) tread | Traction. Recaps are common |
| Trailer | 8 | Trailer (free-rolling) tread | Recaps are common. Many curb and scrub damages |

**Duals:** two tires side by side. **Wide-base singles (super singles):** one wide tire instead of a dual pair; lighter and better mpg, but no "limp" if one fails.

## Reading a tire size
**295/75R22.5**: 295 mm wide, sidewall height is 75% of the width, radial, fits a 22.5-inch wheel. Other common sizes: 11R22.5, 11R24.5, 285/75R24.5, and wide-base 445/50R22.5.

**Load range** (G, H): how much weight it can carry at a given pressure. **DOT code** on the sidewall: the last four digits are the week and year it was made (2523 = week 25 of 2023).

## Tread depth (the legal minimums)
- Steer tires: **4/32 inch**.
- Drive and trailer tires: **2/32 inch**.

Fleets usually pull steer tires at 6/32 to 8/32 and send drive and trailer tires to recap before they go too low, so the casing is still good.

## Pressure
Most truck tires run around **100 to 110 psi** cold (check the tire maker's load chart). Underinflation is the number one killer of tires: it builds heat and ends in a blowout or a "zipper" sidewall failure. A tire run flat on a dual will often damage its partner too. **Tire pressure monitoring** and automatic inflation systems on trailers help.

## Recaps (retreads)
A worn tire's casing is inspected and new tread is bonded on. Costs about half of a new tire. A good casing can be recapped 2 or 3 times. Fleets track their casings; a "casing credit" is paid back for good ones.

## Wheels
- **Steel or aluminum.** Aluminum is lighter and costs more.
- **Hub-piloted** (most today) wheels center on the hub. Lug nuts are torqued to about **450 to 500 ft-lb** and **re-torqued** after 50 to 100 miles when a wheel has been off.
- **Loose wheel signs:** rust streaks from the lug nuts, shiny worn spots. A lost wheel is a serious accident risk.
- **Wheel seals and hubs:** oil-filled or grease-filled hubs. A leaking wheel seal throws oil on the brakes. A failed bearing can lead to a wheel-end fire.

## Alignment
Bad alignment eats steer tires in weeks. Signs: feathered or one-side wear on steer tires, the truck pulling to one side. Alignment checks belong in the PM schedule and after any front-end repair.

## Road service tire calls
1. Unit, position (for example "right rear drive, inside"), size from the sidewall.
2. Is the tire flat, blown, or just low? Is the partner tire damaged?
3. Is the wheel damaged?
4. Safe location? Shoulder, ramp, truck stop.
5. New or recap? Many fleets send recaps for drive and trailer, new for steer.

Road service tires cost more than shop tires, plus a service call fee and mileage. A driver who can limp to the next truck stop safely (on a dual, slowly, after inspection) can save money, but never on a steer tire.

## Tire brands you will hear
Michelin, Bridgestone, Goodyear, Continental, Yokohama, Toyo, plus budget and import brands. Recap brands: Bandag (Bridgestone), Michelin Retread Technologies, Goodyear UniCircle.
