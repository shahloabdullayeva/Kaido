# Cooling and AC
Engine cooling, cab heat and air conditioning.

## Engine cooling
| Part | Job | Trouble |
|---|---|---|
| Radiator | Gets rid of engine heat | Clogged fins (bugs, dirt), leaks, cracked tanks |
| Coolant | Carries heat; protects from freezing and corrosion | Wrong mix, low level, contaminated with oil |
| Water pump | Pushes coolant around | Leak from weep hole, bearing noise |
| Thermostat(s) | Open when the engine is warm | Stuck closed = overheating; stuck open = no heat, poor mpg |
| Fan and fan clutch | Pulls air through the radiator | Fan clutch stuck off = overheats in traffic or on hills |
| Hoses and clamps | Connect everything | Swelling, cracks, loose clamps. A burst hose empties the system in minutes |
| Surge tank and cap | Holds expansion and pressure | Bad cap = coolant loss and boiling |

**Overheating:** stop the truck before damage. Ask the driver for the temperature reading, whether coolant is visible on the ground, and whether the fan is turning. Running an engine hot can warp the head and turn a $300 hose into a $20,000 engine.

**Coolant leak without a visible puddle:** coolant may be going into the oil (oil cooler, head gasket) or into the exhaust (EGR cooler). White sweet-smelling smoke or milky oil are signs. Oil analysis confirms it.

## Cab heat
Heater core in the dash, fed by engine coolant. No heat: low coolant, stuck-open thermostat, blend door actuator, or a clogged heater core. Sleepers have a second heater; many trucks have a fuel-fired bunk heater (Espar, Webasto) for nights without idling.

## Air conditioning
The parts you already know from service cases:
- **Compressor:** belt driven, with an electric clutch. Pumps refrigerant (R-134a on most trucks; R-1234yf on some new ones).
- **Condenser:** in front of the radiator; gets rid of heat.
- **Receiver-drier:** removes moisture; replaced whenever the system is opened.
- **Expansion valve or orifice tube:** drops pressure so the refrigerant gets cold.
- **Evaporator:** in the dash; makes the cold air.
- **Blower motor and resistor:** moves the air. Only some speeds working = resistor.
- **Pressure switches and sensors:** protect the compressor.

Common AC jobs: recharge after a leak test (dye or nitrogen), compressor replacement (often $1,500 to $3,000 installed), condenser damage from road debris, blower motor, clutch coil. Recharging without finding the leak just wastes money; ask the shop whether they leak tested.

Sleeper AC comes from the main system, a separate electric system (with an APU or battery HVAC), or both.

## Seasonal checks
- Before winter: coolant freeze point, batteries, block heater, fuel anti-gel, bunk heater.
- Before summer: AC performance test, condenser and radiator cleaning, fan clutch.

## More detail
The cooling system carries heat away from the engine so it doesn't overheat, and an overheated diesel can destroy its head gasket or crack its head in minutes.

**How it flows.** The **water pump** pushes coolant through channels inside the engine block and head (the water jacket), where it picks up heat. The hot coolant goes out the **upper hose** to the **radiator** at the front of the truck, where air passing through cools it. The cooled coolant comes back through the **lower hose** to the pump, and the loop repeats. The **thermostat** is a valve that stays closed while the engine is cold (so it warms up fast) and opens at operating temperature. Normal running temperature is around 190 to 210°F.

| Part | What it does | Common problems |
|---|---|---|
| Radiator | Cools the coolant with air flowing through its fins | Leaks, clogged fins (bugs, dirt), cracked plastic tanks |
| Water pump (coolant pump) | Circulates coolant | Leaks from the weep hole, bearing noise, fails completely |
| Thermostat | Opens and closes to control engine temperature | Stuck closed = overheating; stuck open = engine runs cold, poor heat in cab |
| Fan and fan clutch | The big fan behind the radiator. The fan clutch engages it only when the engine is hot, to save fuel | Clutch stuck off = overheating in traffic; stuck on = loud and wastes fuel |
| Upper and lower hoses, clamps | Carry coolant between engine and radiator | Cracks, soft spots, leaks at clamps |
| Coolant reservoir (surge tank, expansion tank) | The see through plastic tank where you check and add coolant | Cracks, low level |
| Pressure cap | Keeps the system under pressure so coolant boils at a higher temperature | Bad seal = coolant loss |
| Heater core and heater hoses | A mini radiator that heats the cab | Leaks, no heat |
| Oil cooler | Uses coolant to cool the engine oil | Internal leak mixes oil into coolant |
| EGR cooler | Uses coolant to cool recirculated exhaust | Leaks coolant into the exhaust (white smoke, disappearing coolant) |
| Coolant temperature sensor | Tells the ECM and the dash gauge how hot the engine is | False readings, codes |

**Coolant itself.** Coolant (antifreeze) is usually a 50/50 mix of antifreeze and water. Most modern trucks run **ELC** (extended life coolant), which lasts years if nobody mixes in the wrong type. Mixing coolant types is a classic mistake that causes gel and corrosion.

**Signs of trouble:** temperature gauge climbing, coolant level dropping with no visible leak (think EGR cooler, oil cooler or head gasket), coolant in the oil (milky oil), white sweet smelling smoke, puddles under the front of the truck.

**Maintenance:** keep the coolant at the right mix and replace it on schedule, check the fan and water pump especially when temperatures change, and pressure test the system to find leaks and air pockets.
