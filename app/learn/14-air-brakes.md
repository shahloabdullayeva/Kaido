# Air system and brakes
How air brakes work, the parts, and the inspection items DOT looks at first.

## How air brakes work
Trucks use compressed air, not brake fluid.

![How air moves from the compressor to the brakes](air-system.svg)

1. The **air compressor** (driven by the engine) pumps air.
2. The **air dryer** takes moisture and oil out of it.
3. Air is stored in **tanks (reservoirs)**: primary, secondary, and a wet tank.
4. The **governor** keeps pressure between about **100 and 125 psi** (cut-in and cut-out).
5. When the driver presses the brake pedal (foot valve), air goes to the **brake chambers** at each wheel.
6. The chamber pushes a rod, which turns the **slack adjuster**, which turns the **S-cam**, which pushes the **brake shoes** against the **drum**. Disc brakes (rotors and pads) are common on steer axles of newer trucks.

**Spring brakes (parking and emergency):** strong springs inside the rear chambers apply the brakes when there is no air. Air pressure holds them off. That is why a truck with a big air leak locks its own brakes: it fails safe. The yellow knob in the cab is the tractor park brake; the red knob is the trailer air supply.

## Parts and common faults

![Foundation brake at the wheel: chamber, slack adjuster, S-cam, shoes, drum](brake-wheel.svg)

![Air disc brake on a truck axle](brakes-disc.jpg)

![Air dryer with its cartridge](brakes-dryer.jpg)

| Part | Fault |
|---|---|
| Compressor | Pumping oil, slow build-up, noisy |
| Air dryer and cartridge | Wet air, frozen lines in winter. Cartridge changed on schedule |
| Governor | Wrong cut-in or cut-out pressure |
| Air lines and fittings | Leaks (you can hear them), chafed lines |
| Brake chambers | Broken spring, leaking diaphragm |
| Slack adjusters (automatic) | Out of adjustment. Usually means a worn part, not just an adjustment |
| Shoes and drums | Worn linings, cracked drums, contamination with grease |
| ABS | Wheel speed sensor faults, ABS lamp on |
| Gladhands | The trailer air couplers; seals leak |

## Testing
- **Air leak-down test:** with the engine off and brakes released, pressure should not drop more than 2 psi in a minute for a single truck or 3 psi for a combination. With the brakes held on: 3 psi single, 4 psi combination.
- **Low air warning:** must come on at or above 60 psi.
- **Build-up time:** from 85 to 100 psi in under about 45 seconds.
- **Pushrod stroke:** measured with brakes applied at 90 to 100 psi. Each chamber size has a legal limit (for example a type 30 long-stroke chamber is 2.5 inches). Over the limit = "out of adjustment".

## Why brakes matter so much at DOT
Brake violations are the most common out-of-service reason in roadside inspections. **Brake Safety Week** and similar CVSA events target them every year. More than 20% of brakes out of adjustment on a vehicle puts the whole vehicle out of service.

## Brake jobs you will approve
- **Brake job per axle:** shoes, hardware, sometimes drums. Ask whether drums are being replaced or turned, and why.
- **Chamber replacement:** often both sides of an axle together.
- **Slack adjuster replacement:** if it keeps going out of adjustment.
- **ABS sensor:** common and cheap; cleaning or replacing the sensor at the wheel.
- **Air dryer cartridge:** a PM item.

## More detail

![Air flows from the compressor to every brake chamber](fs-air-flow.png)

Trucks stop with **compressed air**, not brake fluid like a car. Brakes are the single biggest safety item and the biggest focus of DOT inspections, so this is the system to know best.

The compressor fills the tanks, the driver presses the pedal, air rushes into the brake chambers, and the chambers push the brakes on. The same air goes back through the gladhands to brake the trailer too.

## Each part

| Part | What it does | Common problems |
|---|---|---|
| Air compressor | Pump driven by the engine that makes the air | Slow to build pressure, pumping oil into the system, noisy |
| Governor | Tells the compressor when to stop and start pumping | Wrong cut in / cut out pressures |
| Air dryer | Removes water and oil from the air so lines don't rust or freeze | Clogged cartridge, moisture in tanks. Cartridge replaced at PM |
| Air tanks (reservoirs) | Store the air. There's a supply (wet) tank and two service tanks for the primary (rear) and secondary (front) circuits | Water and rust inside, leaks. Drivers drain them with a pull cord or valve |
| Foot valve (brake pedal) | Sends air to the chambers when the driver presses it | Air leaks, weak braking |
| Brake chambers | Round cans at each wheel. Air pushes a rod out of the chamber | Cracked diaphragm = air leak. On drive and trailer axles they are **spring brake** chambers |
| Slack adjuster | Arm that connects the pushrod to the brake and keeps the brakes adjusted | Out of adjustment = weak brakes, a very common DOT violation |
| S cam, shoes and drums | The S cam turns and forces the brake shoes against the drum | Worn shoes (lining), cracked or overheated drums |
| Disc brakes (air disc) | Newer trucks sometimes use pads and rotors instead of drums | Pad wear |
| Air lines and hoses | Carry the air | Chafed, leaking, pinched lines |
| Tractor protection valve | Protects the tractor's air if the trailer breaks away or leaks. Controlled by the red knob in the cab | Sticking valve |
| Gladhands | Couplers joining tractor air to trailer air. **Red** = emergency (supply), **Blue** = service (control) | Bad rubber seals leak |
| ABS (anti lock brakes) | Sensors and valves that stop wheels from locking up | ABS light on, bad wheel sensor |

**Spring brakes, the clever part.** The parking and emergency brakes are huge springs inside the chambers on the drive and trailer axles. **Air holds the springs off.** Push the yellow knob in the cab and air releases the parking brakes; pull it and they apply. If a truck loses all its air, the springs slam the brakes on by themselves. That's why a big air leak doesn't make the truck roll away; it stops it.

**Numbers to know** (from standard CDL pre-trip rules; exact numbers vary slightly by truck)

- Governor cut out (compressor stops): about **120 to 135 psi**. Cut in (starts again): about 20 to 25 psi lower.
- Low air warning buzzer and light: must come on **at or before 60 psi**.
- Spring brakes pop on by themselves around **20 to 45 psi**.
- Air loss with brakes held on: no more than **3 psi per minute** for a single truck, **4 psi per minute** for a combination (tractor + trailer).
- Slack adjuster free play: about **1 inch** when pulled by hand.

**Brake tests the driver does** (they are in the pre-trip guide's last section): the air leakage test, the low air warning test, the spring brake pop out test, the parking brake test (tug against the brakes) and the service brake test (roll slowly and press the pedal).
