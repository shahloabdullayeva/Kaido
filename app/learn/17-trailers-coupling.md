# Trailers, reefers and coupling
How the tractor and trailer connect, and what goes wrong there.

## The connection

![Fifth wheel: the jaws lock in the slot at the center](coupling-fifth.jpg)

![Gladhand air coupler](coupling-gladhand.jpg)

- **Fifth wheel:** the big greased plate on the back of the tractor. Its **jaws** lock around the trailer's **kingpin**.
- **Release handle:** the driver pulls it to unlock the jaws.
- **Slide:** many fifth wheels slide forward and back to balance weight between steer and drive axles. Locked with pins or a plunger.
- **Air lines (gladhands):** red is **emergency/supply**, blue is **service** (the brake signal).
- **Electrical cord:** the 7-way cord for lights and ABS.

## Problems
- **High hook / missed lock:** the jaws closed over the kingpin incorrectly. The trailer can drop. Drivers must do a tug test and look at the jaws.
- **Worn jaws or loose lock:** clunking when starting and stopping. Adjusted or rebuilt.
- **Dry fifth wheel:** needs grease at PMs; a dry plate makes the truck hard to steer.
- **Gladhand seals leaking:** cheap rubber seals; drivers can carry spares.
- **Kingpin wear (on the trailer):** measured with a gauge; worn pins are replaced.

## Trailer landing gear
The legs that hold the front of the trailer when it is unhooked. Crank handle, gearbox, and feet. Bent or broken landing gear is common after hard drops.

## Trailer maintenance
Trailers have no engine, but they have brakes, tires, lights, suspension and doors. A trailer PM covers:
- Brakes (adjustment, linings, chambers, ABS).
- Tires and wheels (tread, pressure, lug nuts, hub oil level).
- Lights, reflective tape, the 7-way plug and wiring.
- Suspension (air bags, springs, the slider pins).
- Doors, hinges, seals, the floor and roof (leaks damage freight).
- Landing gear.
- Mud flaps and the rear impact guard (ICC bumper).

Trailers also need a **DOT annual inspection** each year.

## Reefers
The reefer unit is a small diesel engine running a refrigeration system on the front of the trailer. Main brands: **Thermo King** and **Carrier Transicold**.
- It has its own **fuel tank** (often 50 gallons), oil, filters, belts and battery.
- Service is by **hours**, not miles. The unit shows engine hours on its display.
- **Pre-trip (PTI) on the unit:** a self-test the driver runs before loading.
- **Setpoint:** the temperature the shipper wants, for example 34 °F for produce or -10 °F for frozen.
- **Modes:** continuous (always running) or cycle-sentry/start-stop (runs only when needed; saves fuel).
- **Alarms:** codes on the display. A reefer down with a loaded trailer is an emergency; freight can be rejected for temperature, and claims can be thousands of dollars.
- **Download:** the unit records temperatures. Customers ask for downloads when freight is rejected.

Reefer fuel is often bought on the same card as tractor fuel, at the same pump or a separate reefer pump. That is a common fuel fraud blind spot.

## Who owns the trailer
Many fleets use trailers they do not own: **leased** trailers (from XTRA, Premier and similar companies), **customer** trailers (drop-and-hook programs), or **power only** loads. Repairs on a leased or customer trailer usually need approval from the owner. Always check who owns the trailer before approving work.

## How the tractor and trailer connect (coupling)

- **Fifth wheel:** the plate on the tractor. It has locking **jaws** that close around the trailer's **kingpin**. It needs grease, and the locking mechanism is checked on every pre-trip. A bad lock can let a trailer drop off the truck.
- **Sliding fifth wheel:** can be moved forward or back to balance weight between the steer and drive axles.
- **Gladhands and air lines:** connect tractor air to the trailer (red emergency, blue service).
- **7 way cord:** electrical power for trailer lights and ABS.
- **Landing gear:** the two legs at the front of the trailer that hold it up when no tractor is under it. Cranked up and down by hand.
- **Trailer tandem slider:** the trailer axles can slide forward or back to balance weight, locked with pins.
- **Mud flaps, DOT reflective tape, under ride guard (the rear bumper), and side skirts** (aero panels) are all trailer items that get inspected or damaged.

**Trailer repairs you'll see:** lights and wiring, brakes and slack adjusters, tires, air leaks, landing gear, door hinges and seals, floor damage, reefer unit repairs, and annual inspections (trailers need their own).
