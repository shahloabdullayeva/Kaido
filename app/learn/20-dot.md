# DOT, FMCSA and roadside inspections
The rules, the inspections, out-of-service, and CSA scores.

## Who makes the rules
- **FMCSA** (Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration): federal rules for trucks and drivers, written in the **FMCSRs** (49 CFR parts 390 to 399).
- **CVSA** (Commercial Vehicle Safety Alliance): sets the inspection procedure and the **out-of-service criteria** police use.
- **State police and DOT officers:** do the roadside inspections and run the weigh stations (scales).

Every carrier has a **USDOT number**. Its safety record is public.

## Required maintenance rules (the ones you will work with)
- **Part 396.3:** systematic inspection, repair and maintenance, with records kept for each vehicle.
- **Part 396.11:** drivers must do a post-trip DVIR (driver vehicle inspection report) and list defects. Defects affecting safety must be repaired and the repair certified before the vehicle runs again.
- **Part 396.13:** drivers must check the vehicle before driving and review the last DVIR.
- **Part 396.17:** an **annual inspection** every 12 months by a qualified inspector, with the report kept and proof (sticker or report) on the vehicle.
- **Part 393:** what parts and equipment must be on the truck and work (lights, brakes, tires, mirrors, etc.).

Records: maintenance records are kept for a year while the vehicle is in the fleet and 6 months after it leaves. Annual inspection reports are kept 14 months.

## Roadside inspection levels
| Level | What happens |
|---|---|
| I | Full: driver documents and the whole vehicle, including underneath |
| II | Walk-around: driver and vehicle without going under the truck |
| III | Driver only: license, medical card, hours of service, ELD |
| IV | Special one-off study |
| V | Vehicle only, without the driver present (often at a yard) |
| VI | Radioactive shipments |
| VII | Jurisdiction-specific |
| VIII | Electronic, wireless |

## Out of service (OOS)
If an inspector finds an item on the **CVSA out-of-service criteria**, the truck (or driver) cannot move until it is fixed. Common vehicle OOS items:
- Brakes out of adjustment or not working, air leaks, audible leaks at chambers.
- Tires: flat, exposed cords, tread below the minimum on a steer tire, a tire touching another tire.
- Lights: required lamps not working (at night, a missing headlamp or brake lamp).
- Steering: too much lash (play).
- Suspension: broken leaf spring, cracked frame.
- Cargo securement on flatbeds.
- Fuel or exhaust leaks.

The fleet then has to send road service to fix it on the spot.

## CSA scores and BASICs
**CSA (Compliance, Safety, Accountability)** scores every carrier from inspection and crash data. Violations are grouped into **BASICs**:
- Unsafe Driving
- Hours-of-Service Compliance
- Driver Fitness
- Controlled Substances and Alcohol
- **Vehicle Maintenance** (this is the maintenance department's score)
- Hazardous Materials Compliance
- Crash Indicator

Each violation has a severity weight; newer violations count more. A carrier above the threshold in a BASIC gets an "alert", more inspections, and can lose customers and insurance options. A **clean inspection** (no violations) also helps the score.

## DataQs
If a violation was written wrong, the carrier can challenge it in FMCSA's **DataQs** system with proof (photos, repair invoices, the annual inspection).

## Weigh stations and PrePass
Trucks must stop at open scales unless they have a bypass service (PrePass, Drivewyze). A carrier with a good safety record gets bypassed more often; that is one reason the maintenance score matters.

## Hours of service (so you understand drivers)
- 11 hours driving after 10 hours off.
- 14-hour on-duty window.
- 30-minute break after 8 hours driving.
- 60 hours in 7 days or **70 hours in 8 days**, reset with a **34-hour restart**.

A breakdown eats into the driver's clock even while they wait. Kaido shows these clocks from the ELD on trips.

## More detail
The federal regulator for trucking is **FMCSA** (Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration), part of the US DOT. Its rules (the FMCSRs, in 49 CFR) say trucks must be inspected, repaired and documented. Breaking them means fines, trucks pulled off the road, and a worse safety score that costs the carrier customers and insurance money.

## The inspections

| Inspection | Who does it | How often | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-trip / post-trip | Driver | Every day | Defects go on the DVIR; maintenance fixes them |
| Annual (periodic) DOT inspection | A qualified mechanic or shop | Every 12 months, for each tractor and each trailer | A sticker or certificate stays on the vehicle; the report is kept in the file |
| Roadside inspection | State police or DOT officer, at weigh stations or on the road | Random or targeted | Violations are recorded against the carrier; serious ones put the truck out of service |

**What the annual DOT inspection checks.** The minimum standard is FMCSA's Appendix G to Part 396. The inspector goes through these areas on each tractor and each trailer, and anything that fails has to be fixed before the truck passes:

| Area | What the inspector looks at |
|---|---|
| Brake system | Service and parking brakes work; brake adjustment (pushrod stroke); drums, shoes and linings (thickness, cracks, oil); air hoses and tubing (leaks, chafing); low air warning; tractor protection valve; air compressor; air leakage; ABS warning light |
| Coupling devices | Fifth wheel mounting, locking jaws and release; kingpin and upper coupler; safety chains where used |
| Exhaust system | No leaks under or in front of the cab or sleeper; nothing hot touching wiring, fuel or other burnable parts; secure mounting |
| Fuel system | No leaks; fuel cap present; tanks securely mounted |
| Lighting | Every required light works: headlights, turn signals, brake lights, tail lights, clearance and marker lights, ID lights; reflectors and reflective tape |
| Safe loading | Cargo can't shift into the cab; front end structure and securement points are sound |
| Steering | Steering wheel free play within limits; column, steering gear, pitman arm, drag link, tie rods and ball joints tight, not worn or cracked; power steering works; no missing nuts or cotter pins |
| Suspension | U bolts, leaf springs (no broken or shifted leaves), spring hangers, torque rods, air bags (no leaks); axles held in position |
| Frame | No cracks, breaks or sagging; no parts rubbing the tires; sliding axle frames (trailer slider) locked properly |
| Tires | Steer tires at least 4/32"; others at least 2/32"; no cuts to the cords, bulges, audible leaks or flat tires; no regrooved tires on the steer axle |
| Wheels and rims | No cracks; no loose, missing or broken lug nuts or studs; no unauthorized welds |
| Windshield | No cracks or damage in the driver's view area beyond the allowed limits |
| Windshield wipers | Work properly |

The shop gives a report and a sticker or decal; the report stays in the unit's file for at least 14 months. A PM B or C is often timed to include the annual inspection so the truck only goes in once.

**Roadside inspection levels.** CVSA (the inspectors' association) defines eight levels. The ones you'll hear about: **Level I** is the full inspection of driver and vehicle, including under the truck. **Level II** is a walk around without going underneath. **Level III** is the driver only: license, medical card, hours of service logs. A truck that passes a Level I gets a **CVSA decal**, which often lets it skip inspections for a few months.

**Out of service (OOS).** Some violations are serious enough that the truck (or driver) can't move until fixed, following CVSA's out of service criteria. Common vehicle OOS items: too many brakes out of adjustment, air leaks, flat or badly worn tires, tire tread below minimum, cracked frame, bad steering linkage, missing lights at night. An OOS truck needs a road service repair right there, which is expensive and urgent.

**The most common violations** are lights, tires and brakes. That's why those three get so much attention at every PM and pre-trip.

**CSA (Compliance, Safety, Accountability).** FMCSA's scoring system. Every inspection and crash feeds a carrier's scores in seven categories called **BASICs**: Unsafe Driving, Hours of Service Compliance, Vehicle Maintenance, Controlled Substances and Alcohol, Hazardous Materials Compliance, Driver Fitness, and Crash Indicator. The maintenance team directly controls the **Vehicle Maintenance** score. A bad score brings DOT audits and higher insurance.

**Records to keep:** annual inspection reports, DVIRs, repair and maintenance records for each unit, tire and brake work. DOT can ask for them in an audit.

**Other rules you'll hear: HOS** (hours of service: how long a driver can drive), **ELD** (the electronic logging device that records those hours), **IFTA** (fuel tax reporting, which uses fuel card data by state), **IRP** (registration across states).
