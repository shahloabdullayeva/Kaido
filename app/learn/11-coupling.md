# Fifth wheel and coupling
How the tractor and trailer connect, and what goes wrong there.

## The connection

![Fifth wheel: the jaws lock in the slot at the center](coupling-fifth.jpg)

![Gladhand air coupler](coupling-gladhand.jpg)

- **Fifth wheel:** the big greased plate on the back of the tractor. Its **jaws** lock around the trailer's **kingpin**.
- **Release handle:** the driver pulls it to unlock the jaws.
- **Slide:** many fifth wheels slide forward and back to balance weight between steer and drive axles. Locked with pins or a plunger.
- **Air lines (gladhands):** red is **emergency/supply**, blue is **service** (the brake signal).
- **Electrical cord:** the 7-way cord for lights and ABS.

## Problems
- **High hook / missed lock:** the jaws closed over the kingpin incorrectly. The trailer can drop. Drivers must do a tug test and look at the jaws.
- **Worn jaws or loose lock:** clunking when starting and stopping. Adjusted or rebuilt.
- **Dry fifth wheel:** needs grease at PMs; a dry plate makes the truck hard to steer.
- **Gladhand seals leaking:** cheap rubber seals; drivers can carry spares.
- **Kingpin wear (on the trailer):** measured with a gauge; worn pins are replaced.

## Trailer landing gear
The legs that hold the front of the trailer when it is unhooked. Crank handle, gearbox, and feet. Bent or broken landing gear is common after hard drops.
