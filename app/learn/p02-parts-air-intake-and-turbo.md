# Air intake and turbo
Each part: where it is, what it does, how it fails, and a rough installed price.

## Parts
| Part | Where / what it does | Signs of trouble | Installed (approx) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air filter | Large canister by the hood or behind the cab | Low power, restriction gauge in red | $100 to $250 |
| Air filter restriction gauge | Shows when the filter is clogged | Stuck reading | $30 to $100 |
| Air intake hose and ducting | Filter to turbo | Cracks let dirt in, damaging the engine | $150 to $500 |
| Turbocharger | Exhaust spun fan forcing air into the engine | Whistle, oil leaks, smoke, low power | $1,500 to $3,500 |
| Turbo actuator | Controls boost | Low power, boost codes | $800 to $2,000 |
| Charge air cooler (CAC, intercooler) | Front of the radiator stack; cools air from the turbo | Leaks, low power, black smoke | $1,200 to $3,000 |
| CAC boots and clamps | Rubber joints on the CAC pipes | Blown boot, loud whoosh, big power loss | $150 to $500 |
| Intake manifold | Delivers air into each cylinder | Leaks, cracked | $800 to $2,500 |
| Intake throttle valve | Controls airflow for regens | Stuck, regen codes | $500 to $1,500 |
| Intake air temperature (IAT) sensor | Tells the ECM the air temperature | Codes, poor running | $100 to $300 |
| Boost pressure sensor | Measures turbo boost | Codes, low power | $150 to $400 |
