# Diesel engine basics
Every semi runs on a diesel engine, which is built very differently from a car's gasoline engine: no spark plugs, no carburettor, no distributor.

## Overview
Every semi runs on a diesel engine, which is built very differently from a car's gasoline engine: no spark plugs, no carburettor, no distributor.

## How a diesel makes power, in plain words

Inside the engine are **cylinders** (heavy trucks have 6, in a straight line). In each cylinder a **piston** moves up and down. The piston squeezes air so hard that the air gets extremely hot. Then a **fuel injector** sprays diesel into that hot air, and it ignites by itself from the heat. The explosion pushes the piston down. Pistons are connected to the **crankshaft**, which turns that up and down motion into spinning, and the spinning goes to the wheels through the drivetrain.

That is why diesel engines need so much air, so much pressure, and so much cooling. Almost every system in the next sections exists to feed this process air, fuel, oil and coolant, or to clean up the exhaust after.

## Engine makers

- **Detroit Diesel** (DD13, DD15, DD16): Freightliner and Western Star. The DD15 is the most common engine in US fleets. The number is roughly the size in liters.
- **Cummins X15**: offered in almost every brand. Big independent maker.
- **PACCAR MX-13, MX-11**: Peterbilt and Kenworth.
- **Volvo D13, D11**: Volvo. **Mack MP7, MP8**: Mack.
- **International S13, A26**: International.

## The main parts of the engine itself

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Engine block | The big metal body that holds the cylinders |
| Cylinder head | Sits on top of the block, holds the valves and injectors. A cracked head is a very expensive repair |
| Head gasket | The seal between block and head. A blown one mixes oil and coolant |
| Pistons, rods, crankshaft | The moving parts that turn fuel into rotation |
| Camshaft and valves | Open and close to let air in and exhaust out at the right moment |
| Oil pan | The tank at the bottom of the engine that holds the oil |
| Flywheel | Heavy wheel at the back of the engine that connects to the clutch |
| ECM (Engine Control Module) | The engine's computer. Controls fuel, air and emissions, stores fault codes, and can limit speed |

## Words you'll hear

- **In frame overhaul / out of frame overhaul:** rebuilding the engine while it sits in the truck, or pulling it out. Huge jobs, often $20,000 or more.
- **Blow by:** combustion gas leaking past worn piston rings. Sign of a tired engine.
- **Engine brake / Jake brake:** uses the engine to slow the truck so the regular brakes don't overheat on hills.
