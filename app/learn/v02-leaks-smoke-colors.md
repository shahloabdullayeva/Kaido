# Leaks and smoke colors
The color of a puddle or of exhaust smoke tells you which fluid or problem it is.
## Leaks by color
Have the driver put a white paper towel in the drip and describe the color, feel and smell. The table gives the usual answer.
| Color | Feels and smells like | What fluid it is | Where it usually comes from | Urgency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| {{#5a3a1a}} Dark brown to black | Slippery, smells of burnt oil | Engine oil | Oil pan gasket, filter, valve cover, turbo oil lines, rear main seal | Check level. Drive if dripping slowly and level is fine; stop if pouring |
| {{#b98a2e}} Amber to honey | Slippery, almost no smell | New engine oil, or hydraulic oil | Fresh oil change leak, filter not tight, hydraulic lines | As above |
| {{#e98aa8}} Pink | Slightly sweet, thin | Extended-life coolant (some brands) | Water pump, hoses, radiator, heater hoses | Soon. Coolant level drops fast |
| {{#e3762c}} Orange | Sweet, thin | Extended-life coolant (Cummins, Detroit, Volvo/Mack OAT) | Water pump weep hole, radiator, hose clamps | Soon |
| {{#56c25a}} Bright green | Sweet, thin | Older conventional coolant | Same as above | Soon |
| {{#e5d24a}} Yellow-green | Sweet | Some fleet coolants | Same as above | Soon |
| {{#9c2a2a}} Red | Oily, slight smell | Automatic transmission fluid (Allison), power steering fluid on many trucks | Transmission cooler lines, steering box, steering pump | Check level. Steering fluid loss is a safety issue |
| {{#c8a74c}} Light brown, thick, strong smell | Gear oil (rotten egg smell) | Differential or manual transmission | Axle pinion seal, wheel seal on oil-bath hubs, transmission seal | Wheel seal leak on brakes: today |
| {{#c4c97b}} Clear to pale yellow, very thin | Strong diesel smell, evaporates slowly | Diesel fuel | Fuel lines, filter housing, tank straps rubbing, crossover line | Large leak: stop. Fuel on hot exhaust is a fire |
| {{#eef5ff}} Clear, no smell, crystals when dry | Like water, leaves white crust | DEF | DEF lines, pump, tank sender, doser | Soon. Truck derates when the tank runs low |
| {{#f5f5f5}} Clear, no smell | Water | AC condensate under the cab, or water from air tanks | Normal | None |
| {{#8a5b2c}} Rusty brown water | Watery | Old coolant or water | Neglected cooling system | Plan a flush |
| {{#7b6a54}} Milky tan sludge | Thick | Oil and coolant mixed | Oil cooler, head gasket, EGR cooler | Stop. Engine damage risk |
| {{#3b3b3b}} Black oily mist on wheel | Greasy streaks on the rim | Hub oil or grease | Wheel seal | Today: oil on brake linings ruins braking |
> stop: Fuel or oil dripping onto the exhaust, turbo or DPF is a fire risk. The driver should shut down and move away from the truck if it smokes.
## What coolant crust looks like
Coolant that leaks slowly dries into a crust. Pink or orange coolant leaves a pink or orange-white crust; green leaves a white-green crust. Look at hose ends, the water pump weep hole (a small hole under the pump pulley), radiator tank seams and the overflow tank. A crust is a slow leak that needs a shop at the next service, not a stop now.
## What DEF crust looks like
DEF dries into white crystals, like salt or frost. You will see it at DEF line fittings, the doser in the exhaust pipe, and the tank cap. A small white bloom at the doser is common. Large crystal build-up blocks the doser and causes DEF codes and derates.
## Exhaust smoke by color
| Color | Looks like | What it means | Usual causes | Urgency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| {{#1c1c1c}} Black | Dark puffs when pulling away or climbing, soot on the trailer front | Too much fuel for the air | Boost leak (cracked charge air cooler or loose clamp), dirty air filter, turbo damage, leaking injector, EGR stuck open | Soon. On a modern truck with a DPF you should never see black smoke: the DPF itself may be cracked |
| {{#f4f4f4}} White, thick, sweet smell, does not clear when warm | Clouds like steam that hang | Coolant burning | EGR cooler leak, head gasket, cracked head | Stop if coolant level drops. Engine damage risk |
| {{#e9ece6}} White-grey, sharp raw fuel smell, burns the eyes | Fog, mostly cold starts | Unburned fuel | Cold engine, bad injector, low compression, intake heater, timing | Watch. If it does not clear when warm: shop |
| {{#d9dde3}} Thin white wisps on a cold morning that clear in a minute | Steam | Water condensing in a cold exhaust | Normal | None |
| {{#7d8aa8}} Blue-grey | Bluish haze, smells of burnt oil | Oil burning | Turbo seals, worn rings or liners, valve guides, too much oil, crankcase breather | Soon. Watch oil level |
| {{#d6d0c2}} White with a sweet ammonia smell after regen | Light haze | DEF or regen by-products | Over-dosing DEF, doser leaking | Soon |
> tip: A modern truck (2010 and newer) with working aftertreatment shows almost no smoke at all. Any visible smoke out of the stack after warm-up means something upstream is wrong.
