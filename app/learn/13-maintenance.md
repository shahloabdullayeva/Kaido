# Preventive maintenance
PM-A, B and C, intervals, what is checked, and how to run a PM program.

## Why PMs matter
A breakdown on the road costs about **3 to 5 times** what the same repair costs in a shop, plus downtime, a late load, and sometimes a towing bill. A good PM program catches problems before they strand a truck. It is also a legal requirement: FMCSA expects "systematic inspection, repair and maintenance" with records.

## PM levels
Names vary by fleet, but the idea is the same.

| Level | Typical interval | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| PM-A | Every 15,000 to 25,000 mi | Oil and oil filter, chassis grease, inspection of brakes, tires, lights, leaks, belts, hoses, fluid levels, air filter restriction |
| PM-B | Every second or third A | A plus fuel filters, air dryer cartridge, coolant test, more detailed brake and steering checks, sometimes alignment check |
| PM-C | Yearly or every 100,000+ mi | B plus valve adjustment (overhead), transmission and axle oil on schedule, coolant service, DOT annual inspection often done together |

Engine makers allow long oil intervals (up to 50,000 to 75,000 mi on some engines in good highway duty), but many fleets keep **grease and inspection** every 15,000 to 25,000 miles because chassis parts need it more often than oil does.

## What a PM inspection covers
- **Engine:** oil, coolant, leaks, belts, hoses, air intake, exhaust leaks, fault codes.
- **Brakes:** stroke on every chamber, linings, drums, air leaks, compressor build-up, ABS.
- **Tires:** tread depth on each position, pressure, damage, matching duals, lug nuts.
- **Steering:** play at the wheel, tie rod ends, kingpins, drag link.
- **Suspension:** air bags, shocks, springs, U-bolts, torque rods.
- **Drivetrain:** U-joints, driveshaft, axle seals, transmission leaks.
- **Electrical:** lights, battery test, charging voltage.
- **Cab:** wipers, horn, mirrors, seat belts, fire extinguisher, triangles, gauges, HVAC.
- **Fifth wheel:** lock, jaws, grease, mounting bolts.
- **Aftertreatment:** DEF level, soot load, last regen, leaks.

## Other scheduled items
- **DOT annual inspection:** every 12 months, tractor and trailer separately.
- **Valve adjustment (overhead):** usually every 300,000 to 500,000 mi depending on engine.
- **DPF cleaning:** by restriction or by mileage.
- **Coolant extender and coolant change.**
- **Transmission and axle oil changes.**
- **APU and reefer services by hours.**
- **Registration, permits, insurance and IFTA decals:** not mechanical, but expired paperwork stops a truck just as surely.

## Running the program
1. **Know every unit's mileage.** Telematics (Samsara) supplies it daily. Kaido syncs it.
2. **Set intervals per truck or per model.** Kaido's truck pages hold oil, PM-A and PM-B intervals.
3. **Schedule ahead.** Book the PM when the truck will be near a shop or home, around 1,000 to 2,000 miles before due.
4. **Track PM compliance:** the share of PMs done on time. Good fleets aim for 90%+.
5. **Close the loop:** every PM creates a work order with the invoice and the findings. Repeat findings show which trucks or parts are a problem.

## Shop or mobile for PMs
- **Dealer:** knows the brand, has warranty access, most expensive.
- **Independent shop:** cheaper, quality varies.
- **Truck stop service (Love's Truck Care, TA Truck Service, Speedco):** fast, drivers can stop on the route. Good for oil changes and quick PMs.
- **Mobile PM:** the mechanic comes to the yard. Good for fleets with a home yard.
