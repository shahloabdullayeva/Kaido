# Cooling system
Each part: where it is, what it does, how it fails, and a rough installed price.

## Parts
| Part | Where / what it does | Signs of trouble | Installed (approx) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radiator | Very front, behind the grille | Leaks, clogged fins, overheating | $1,500 to $3,500 |
| Radiator cap / pressure cap | Keeps system pressure | Coolant loss, overheating | $30 to $80 part |
| Coolant reservoir (surge tank) | Clear plastic tank to check and fill | Cracks, low level | $300 to $800 |
| Upper and lower radiator hoses | Engine to radiator | Cracks, soft spots, leaks | $150 to $450 each |
| Heater hoses | Engine to heater core | Leaks | $100 to $300 |
| Hose clamps (constant tension) | Hold hoses on | Seepage at connections | $10 to $30 each |
| Water pump | Circulates coolant, belt or gear driven | Leak from weep hole, noise | $800 to $1,500 |
| Thermostat(s) and housing | Control engine temperature | Overheating or running cold | $200 to $500 |
| Fan | Big fan behind the radiator | Cracked blades | $300 to $800 |
| Fan clutch (fan hub) | Turns the fan on when hot | Always on (loud) or never on (overheats) | $500 to $1,500 |
| Fan shroud | Guides air through the radiator | Cracked, fan hitting it | $200 to $600 |
| Coolant temperature sensor | Engine temperature for ECM and gauge | False readings | $100 to $300 |
| Coolant level sensor | Warns of low coolant | False low coolant warning | $100 to $300 |
| Coolant filter | Some engines filter coolant | Changed at PM | $30 to $80 part |
| Coolant (ELC or conventional) | Carries heat | Wrong mix, contamination | Flush $200 to $400 |
| Heater core | Behind the dash, heats the cab | No heat, sweet smell, wet floor | $800 to $2,000 (dash work) |
| Transmission cooler | Cools transmission oil | Leaks | $400 to $1,200 |
