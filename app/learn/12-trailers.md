# Trailers and reefers
Trailer types, their maintenance, and refrigeration units.

## Trailer types
| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| Dry van | Closed box. The most common trailer, usually 53 ft |
| Reefer | Insulated box with a refrigeration unit on the front |
| Flatbed / step deck | Open deck for steel, lumber, machinery. Needs straps, chains, tarps |
| Tanker | Liquids, food grade or hazmat |
| Intermodal chassis | Carries shipping containers |
| Lowboy | Heavy equipment |

## Trailer maintenance
Trailers have no engine, but they have brakes, tires, lights, suspension and doors. A trailer PM covers:
- Brakes (adjustment, linings, chambers, ABS).
- Tires and wheels (tread, pressure, lug nuts, hub oil level).
- Lights, reflective tape, the 7-way plug and wiring.
- Suspension (air bags, springs, the slider pins).
- Doors, hinges, seals, the floor and roof (leaks damage freight).
- Landing gear.
- Mud flaps and the rear impact guard (ICC bumper).

Trailers also need a **DOT annual inspection** each year.

## Reefers
The reefer unit is a small diesel engine running a refrigeration system on the front of the trailer. Main brands: **Thermo King** and **Carrier Transicold**.
- It has its own **fuel tank** (often 50 gallons), oil, filters, belts and battery.
- Service is by **hours**, not miles. The unit shows engine hours on its display.
- **Pre-trip (PTI) on the unit:** a self-test the driver runs before loading.
- **Setpoint:** the temperature the shipper wants, for example 34 °F for produce or -10 °F for frozen.
- **Modes:** continuous (always running) or cycle-sentry/start-stop (runs only when needed; saves fuel).
- **Alarms:** codes on the display. A reefer down with a loaded trailer is an emergency; freight can be rejected for temperature, and claims can be thousands of dollars.
- **Download:** the unit records temperatures. Customers ask for downloads when freight is rejected.

Reefer fuel is often bought on the same card as tractor fuel, at the same pump or a separate reefer pump. That is a common fuel fraud blind spot.

## Who owns the trailer
Many fleets use trailers they do not own: **leased** trailers (from XTRA, Premier and similar companies), **customer** trailers (drop-and-hook programs), or **power only** loads. Repairs on a leased or customer trailer usually need approval from the owner. Always check who owns the trailer before approving work.
