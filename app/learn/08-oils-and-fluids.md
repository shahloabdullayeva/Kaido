# Oils and fluids
Every system that moves has its own fluid, and using the wrong one is a classic expensive mistake.

## Overview
Every system that moves has its own fluid, and using the wrong one is a classic expensive mistake. A heavy truck engine holds roughly **10 to 15 gallons** of oil, which is why a truck oil change costs far more than a car's.

## Engine oil: what the labels mean

A jug of diesel oil shows two things: the **API category** (the quality standard) and the **viscosity grade** (the thickness).

| API category | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CK-4 | The current standard for heavy diesel oil (since 2016) | Works in old and new engines. The safe default |
| FA-4 | Thinner oil for better fuel mileage | Only for newer engines (2017 and later) whose maker approves it. Not for older trucks |
| CJ-4 | The older standard | Replaced by CK-4; you still see it on old jugs |
| PC-12 | The next standard, expected with 2027 emission rules | Watch for it on new trucks |

| Grade | Meaning | Where used |
|---|---|---|
| 15W-40 | The classic truck oil. "15W" is how it flows cold (W = winter), "40" is how thick it stays hot | Most common, especially older trucks |
| 10W-30 | Thinner, better fuel mileage | Many newer engines (Detroit, Cummins, Volvo) |
| 5W-40 | Full synthetic, flows well in deep cold | Cold climates, extended drains |
| 10W-30 or 5W-30 FA-4 | Thin fuel economy oil | Only where the engine maker allows FA-4 |

**Conventional vs synthetic.** Conventional oil is refined from crude. Synthetic is engineered, lasts longer, handles heat and cold better, and costs more. Synthetic blend is a mix. Newer trucks often use synthetic or a blend so they can go longer between oil changes.

**Engine maker approvals.** Each engine maker publishes its own oil spec, printed on the back of the jug:

| Engine | CK-4 spec | FA-4 spec |
|---|---|---|
| Detroit (DD13, DD15, DD16) | DFS 93K222 | DFS 93K223 |
| Cummins (X15) | CES 20086 | CES 20087 |
| Volvo (D11, D13) | VDS-4.5 | VDS-5 |
| Mack (MP7, MP8) | EOS-4.5 | EOS-5 |

**Common products:** Shell Rotella T4 (15W-40 conventional), T5 (10W-30 synthetic blend), T6 (5W-40 full synthetic); Chevron Delo 400; Mobil Delvac 1300 Super and Delvac 1; Valvoline Premium Blue. Love's and Speedco stock 15W-40, 10W-30 and 5W-40 that meet CK-4.

**Filters changed at PM:** oil filter (usually one full flow, sometimes a bypass filter too), two fuel filters (primary with water separator, and secondary), and at longer intervals the air filter and air dryer cartridge. Common filter brands: Fleetguard, Donaldson, Baldwin, and each engine maker's own brand.

## Other fluids

| Fluid | Where | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Coolant (antifreeze) | Cooling system | Two families: **ELC** (extended life, lasts years, no additives needed) and **conventional** (needs SCA additives, tested for nitrite level at PM). Never mix them. Mixed about 50/50 with water |
| Gear oil | Differentials (rear axles) | Usually 75W-90 synthetic or 80W-90 / 85W-140 conventional, rated GL-5 |
| Transmission oil | Manual and automated manual gearboxes | Eaton manuals use a 50 weight synthetic transmission fluid; Detroit DT12, Volvo I-Shift and others each have their own approved oil |
| Automatic transmission fluid | Allison automatics | Allison approved ATF (TES-295) |
| Power steering fluid | Steering gear | Often an ATF type fluid; check the cap or manual |
| Grease | Every grease fitting, fifth wheel | NLGI #2 grade chassis grease, applied at every PM A |
| DEF | Blue cap tank | 32.5% urea and water, must meet ISO 22241. Freezes at about 12°F (the truck heats it). Contaminated or diluted DEF causes derates |
| Washer fluid | Windshield | Winter mix in cold states |

Trucks don't use brake fluid for the main brakes, since the brakes run on air.
