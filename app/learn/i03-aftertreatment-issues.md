# Aftertreatment issues
DPF, DEF, SCR, doser and NOx sensors: the problems that cause most derates on modern trucks.
## How a derate builds up
1. A fault is found: amber check engine and a DPF or DEF lamp.
2. After a set time or distance with the fault still there: MIL on, and a first derate (power cut, often a 55 mph limit).
3. Keep driving: final derate, 5 mph limit after a key-off or a refuel. Truck needs a tow or mobile repair.
> warn: The timing is different on each engine and model year. Some go from first warning to 5 mph in 3-4 hours of driving. Treat any DEF or DPF warning as a "today" problem.
## DPF and regen
::: issue DPF soot level high | soon
dash: DPF lamp solid. Center screen says regen needed or soot level high.
why: Lots of idling and short trips, regen interrupted, regen switch set to inhibit, high oil use, leaking injector.
do: Parked (stationary) regen: park away from anything that burns, set the parking brake, press the regen switch, engine speeds up for 20-60 minutes. Do not drive off until done.
:::
::: issue DPF lamp flashing | soon
dash: DPF lamp flashing, often check engine and a first derate.
why: Soot very high. Parked regen still possible on most trucks.
do: Parked regen now. If it will not start or fails, shop: forced regen with a laptop, or a DPF cleaning.
:::
::: issue Regen will not start or keeps failing | soon
looks: Driver presses the switch and nothing happens, or the regen stops after a few minutes.
why: Conditions not met (engine too cold, parking brake not set, clutch or pedals touched), active faults blocking regen (sensors, doser, 7th injector), DOC face plugged, temperature sensors failed.
check: Codes. Common blockers: exhaust temperature sensor, differential pressure sensor (SPN 3251), doser (Detroit SPN 4077/5456), DOC inlet temperature.
do: Shop with a laptop. Fix the blocker, then a forced regen.
:::
::: issue DPF plugged with ash | soon
looks: Frequent regens, high back pressure, low power. Regens do not bring the soot reading down.
why: Ash from oil builds up and never burns. Every DPF needs cleaning around 200,000-400,000 miles. Earlier if the engine burns oil.
do: DPF clean (bake and air pulse) $400-900, or swap for a clean exchange unit $1,200-2,000 installed.
:::
::: issue Cracked or melted DPF | soon
looks: Black soot coming out of the stack, soot at the DPF outlet clamp. On inspection: cracks or melted spots on the white ceramic face.
why: Uncontrolled regen from too much soot, oil or fuel reaching the DPF (turbo or injector failure).
do: Replace DPF ($2,500-6,000). Find what caused it first or it happens again.
:::
::: issue DPF differential pressure sensor fault | soon
dash: SPN 3251 or similar. Check engine and sometimes a derate.
looks: Cracked or melted rubber hoses to the sensor, sensor tube plugged with soot.
do: Replace hoses ($50-150) or sensor ($150-400). Often a quick fix.
:::
## DEF and SCR
::: issue DEF tank low | soon
dash: DEF gauge low, DEF lamp. Under about 5% the derate countdown starts.
do: Fill DEF. Only use ISO 22241 DEF from sealed jugs or pumps. Never put anything else in the blue cap tank.
:::
::: issue Wrong fluid in the DEF tank | stop
looks: Driver put diesel, coolant or water in the DEF tank. DEF smells wrong or is colored.
dash: DEF quality codes (SPN 3364), derate soon after.
do: Do not run the engine. Drain and flush tank, lines, pump; sometimes replace the doser and SCR catalyst. $1,500-10,000+ depending on how far it got.
:::
::: issue Poor DEF quality code | soon
dash: SPN 3364 (DEF quality), 3516 (concentration). Check engine, DEF lamp.
why: Diluted DEF (rain or water got in), old DEF that sat in heat, contaminated jug, failed quality sensor.
check: Refractometer reading. Good DEF is 32.5%.
do: Drain and refill with fresh DEF. Replace the sensor if DEF tests good.
:::
::: issue DEF doser plugged or crystallized | soon
looks: White crystal crust at the doser in the exhaust pipe, or a hard white lump inside the pipe near the doser.
dash: Doser and DEF pressure codes, low SCR efficiency (SPN 4364, 4094).
why: Doser stuck, DEF heater failed in cold, short trips.
do: Clean or replace the doser ($400-1,200 installed). Clean crystal build-up in the pipe.
:::
::: issue DEF pump or heater failure | soon
dash: DEF pressure codes, DEF heater codes in winter (SPN 3361, 4334, 5246 family).
looks: DEF frozen in the tank in cold weather; lines brittle.
do: Pump $800-2,500 installed. Heater element or relay cheaper.
:::
::: issue NOx sensor failure | soon
dash: SPN 3216 (inlet NOx sensor), 3226 (outlet NOx sensor), sometimes SCR efficiency codes (SPN 4364).
why: The most common aftertreatment part to fail. Heat, age, wiring.
do: Replace sensor $400-900 installed. Use OEM or a quality brand; cheap sensors fail fast.
:::
::: issue Low SCR efficiency | soon
dash: SPN 4364 FMI 18 or similar: the outlet NOx is too high.
why: Bad DEF, failed doser, NOx sensor drift, SCR catalyst coated (sulfur or DEF deposits), exhaust leak before the sensor.
do: Shop. Often a DEF flush plus a new doser or sensor. Catalyst replacement is $3,000-8,000.
:::
## Diesel oxidation catalyst (DOC) and hydrocarbon doser
::: issue DOC face plugged | soon
looks: Black, wet soot caked on the front of the DOC brick.
dash: Regen failures, high DOC inlet temperature.
why: Fuel dosing too much (leaking 7th injector or hydrocarbon doser), lots of idling.
do: Clean or replace DOC ($1,500-3,500). Fix the leaking doser.
:::
::: issue Hydrocarbon doser (7th injector) leaking | soon
looks: Fuel smell at the exhaust, wet soot at the doser, white smoke at cold start.
dash: Detroit SPN 4077, 5456; Cummins and Volvo similar.
do: Replace doser $500-1,500.
:::
